Julius Zhang

Ph.D. student, Computer Science
Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences, New York University

I do research at the intersection of systems, security, and verifiable computation. I am particularly interested in succinct proofs for real-world computations. More broadly, I like problems in systems where computation meets mathematics, across areas such as distributed protocols, convex optimization, and high-performance computing.

I am visiting the Department of Mathematics at Columbia University, hosted by Andrew Blumberg.

I earned my B.A. in Mathematics and M.S. in Computer Science from Stanford University, supported by the General Wang Yaowu Scholarship, where I researched algebraic geometry and geometric topology, especially Floer homotopy theory, and worked on distributed systems.

I write a technical blog on mathematics and computer science as I figure things out.

Outside of research, I like to do judo and sketch in art galleries – a habit from my teenage years in London, when I volunteered at the National Portrait Gallery by Trafalgar Square. I am yondan in Go.
